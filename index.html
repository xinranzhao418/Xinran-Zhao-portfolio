Marketing Analytics Case Study

Google Merchandise Store Marketing Analytics Dashboard

GA4 + Looker Studio analysis of ecommerce acquisition, engagement, channel quality, user behavior, product performance, and conversion opportunities.

View Dashboard
258,614 active users 329,142 sessions $584,049.24 purchase revenue

Key Insights

  1. Direct was the main revenue driver. Direct brought the highest traffic volume with 209,812 sessions and also generated the most purchase revenue at $324,530.56. Organic Search was the second strongest channel, with 73,024 sessions and $144,040.07 in revenue.
  2. Traffic volume and engagement quality did not always move together. Direct had the most sessions, but its engagement rate was only 24.44%. Smaller channels such as Organic Shopping and Cross-network had much higher engagement rates, which shows why channel performance should not be judged by traffic volume alone.
  3. Desktop users drove most of the revenue. Mobile had more sessions than desktop, but desktop generated far more purchase revenue: $555,838.20 compared with $27,753.99 from mobile. This suggests that mobile behavior is worth reviewing more closely, especially around product browsing and checkout.
  4. Ecommerce activity dropped sharply before purchase. The commerce interaction funnel showed 242,145 view_item events, 39,799 add_to_cart events, 12,273 begin_checkout events, and 3,453 purchases. This points to a large drop-off between product views, cart activity, and completed purchases.

Marketing Recommendations

  1. Keep Direct and Organic Search as priority channels. These two channels are carrying most of the revenue, so they should stay central in performance monitoring. For Organic Search, the next step would be to review which landing pages and product pages are helping convert search traffic.
  2. Do not evaluate channels by sessions alone. Some smaller channels had stronger engagement rates than Direct. These channels may be worth testing with more targeted content, remarketing, or campaign support before assuming they are less valuable because of lower traffic.
  3. Review the mobile shopping experience. Mobile brought a large amount of traffic but much lower revenue than desktop. I would not assume the issue is UX without more evidence, but it is worth checking mobile product pages, cart behavior, page speed, and checkout flow.
  4. Use product performance to guide merchandising tests. High-revenue products should be used in homepage placement, email, or remarketing tests. Products with many views but fewer purchases should be reviewed for pricing, product-page content, or checkout friction.
Content Marketing Funnel Case Study

Bootcamp GIS Content Marketing Funnel Case Study

During my marketing internship at Bootcamp GIS, I supported a content workflow that connected SEO/GEO article optimization, WordPress publishing, LinkedIn distribution, course CTA messaging, and performance tracking. The work helped me see content less as a single post and more as a path from search intent to audience trust and next-step messaging.

30% AI search visibility increase 301 LinkedIn impressions 101 video views 34m watch time 39% senior-level viewers

What I Worked On

  1. I used keyword research to check search intent around GIS careers, certification, remote GIS jobs, and AI-related mapping topics.
  2. I updated article structure with clearer headings, TL;DR sections, FAQ-style answers, internal links, source references, and WordPress formatting checks.
  3. I helped prepare LinkedIn posts and group distribution so course-related content could reach GIS, career, and technology audiences.
  4. For a LinkedIn roast video, I edited for pacing by opening with the strongest moment, trimming slower explanations, and keeping the structure easy to follow for someone scrolling through LinkedIn.

What I Learned

  1. Search intent should shape article structure. A stronger article is not just cleaner writing; it is easier to scan, index, and connect to a next step.
  2. Distribution works better when the message fits the audience. I shared the LinkedIn post across GIS Professional & Networking, GIS Jobs, GIS Group, and Esri Connected to reach a more relevant professional audience.
  3. Instructor credibility matters for technical training. Posts felt stronger when they connected a real industry trend to the instructor's practical experience and the course outcome.
  4. The LinkedIn video reached 301 impressions, 179 members, 101 video views, and 34 minutes of watch time in the first few days, with 39% of viewers at a senior level and 21% from IT services and consulting.

Original Douyin Account

What I Built

  3. A July fitness post reached 16.5K views, 1,707 likes, 169 shares, 114 saves, a 52.61% swipe rate, and a 50% cover click rate.
